# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project

"Fuzzy Barnacle" is an iOS app (SwiftUI + SwiftData), deployment target iOS 26.5. It is a **generative art piece**: a body of water, and the lives on and in it.

- **The water**: a tide drives the motes, the light shafts, and the plume sway. On the water's own 1440 s clock there is a day and a night (`ContentView.daylight`); in the night the colony is lit by its own faint glow, and the hand is the only lamp there is, unless the moon is crossing. The sky's light from above has a **color**, not only a brightness: it comes down **warm gold at the water's turning** (the low light) and **white at the high** (`ContentView.skyWarmth` = `exp(−((daylight−0.30)/0.22)²/2)` — a warmth that peaks at the turning, where the light is low, and is gone at the high and nearly gone in the deep night; `ContentView.skyLightRGB`/`skyLightColor` map it to a color, warm gold `(1.0, 0.66, 0.52)` at the turning, white at the high). `drawWater` feeds that color to everything the sky's light touches — the soft surface light, the two light shafts, and the motes (all were plain white; at the high they still render white exactly as before, so a full day is unchanged). The piece's own small lights keep their own colors (the deep's pale blue, the twin's paler blue, the colony's cold glow, the wake's cyan) — only the sky's light now carries a hue. And the caption now **names the turning** (`waterLine` returns "the water is in its turning" in the [0.25, 0.75] band, where it was once silent): "the water is in its night" (light < 0.25), "the water is in its turning" (0.25–0.75), "the water is in its day" (light > 0.75). A pure function of t — the sky does not remember the turning; verify with the shifted clock. And **the water itself drinks the sky's color** (`ContentView.waterColor(t, light:)` — the water's own base depth gradient is multiplied by `skyLightRGB(t)`: at the high the light is white and the water's own blue is exactly as it was, so a full day is unchanged; at the turning the warm light gilds it, the way the sea is gilded at dusk — copper where the light lies, teal where it does not; in the deep night the sky has little light left to give a color, and the water is nearly dark. The water only drinks — its channels never exceed the color of the water with no sky's color in it — and when the turning turns to the day the water is the water again, and does not remember the gold).
- **The colony**: barnacles settle on a tap (persisted as `Barnacle` in SwiftData), accrete to adult size, and age. Holding one prys it off, leaving a `Ghost` — a trace that holds, then dissolves and is pruned.
- **The passing**: five "drifters" ride the current and never settle (`drifterRawPosition` — deterministic from the water's clock, stored nowhere).
- **The storm**: rare weather — two incommensurate currents must align while a slow season is willing (`ContentView.storm`); then the current surges, rain falls, the light dims under the cloud, the colony tucks in, and when it passes the water does not remember it. The storm's surge also bends the water around the deep bodies' backs more than calm water would — the way a flood bends more around a stone (`deepPartingStrength` = 9.0·deep·(1+0.5·storm), `deepTwinPartingStrength` = 7.0·twin·(1+0.5·storm) in `drawWater`; the rain falls on the surface's answer, the colony tucks and slows its breath under a passing body at the same time, and the body's tone keeps its own breath through the sky's dark word — the deep one does not read the sky's word). The storm's voice has a **low end** — the roll under the rain (`ContentView.rollGain` = 0.035·storm·(0.6+0.4·light), a little less in the night, the way the rain is a little less in the night): in `WaterVoice` its own draw of the water's brown noise, low-passed to the deep (90 Hz), its amplitude rolling within the storm on two incommensurate swells (5.3 s / 13.9 s, shaped 0.3–1.0 — the lull keeps its low hum, the way a storm never fully goes quiet), gain easing 0.002/sample: the sky's dark word's low end, the storm's voice in the deep, very low and very slow — the low end never drowns the surface's own voice (the roll at most 0.035, the rain at most 0.16) and keeps below the body's own voice (0.035 < 0.05, the body's tone heard above the roll, the way the sky's word keeps below the body's); when the storm passes the roll goes with it, and the water does not remember it.
- **The moon**: the sky's own slow word — rare, and kept to the night. Two incommensurate currents (1723 s / 791 s) must align while a slower clear-sky season (4871 s) is willing (`ContentView.moon`); `moonDrawn` multiplies by `1 − daylight`, so a crossing in the day passes unseen. Rarer than the storm — ~27 windows in 48 h. While a crossing is over the water, a broad cold beam drifts across it (`moonBeamX` — an 887 s drift; `moonBeamSigma` 0.16·W; `moonBeamFall`): the colony's halos take the sky's light over their own, plumes reach into the passing light, the motes catch the beam, and the traces and the passing are lit. The caption keeps it (`moonLine`: "the moon is over the water" at m > 0.15, "the moon crosses the water" at m > 0.5). A pure function of t — the water does not remember the crossing; verify with the shifted clock.
- **The deep one**: the piece's first body of its own — not the water's, not the colony's, not the sky's: something that most rarely of all passes through the deep. Two incommensurate currents (2531 s / 1117 s) must align while a slow season (5999 s) is willing (`ContentView.deep`); rarer than the moon — ~24 windows in 48 h (the moon's ~52), calm 97 % of the time; passages last 90–190 s. It drifts fully off both sides of the water (`deepX` — a 1433 s drift; `deepBodyLength` 0.55·W) and stays deep (`deepY` — 0.80 ± 0.045 of the height). The current goes around its back (`bodyParting` — the generalized bend that turns the water around the hand's rock: the motes part, go around, and close behind it; `deepPartingStrength` = 9.0·deep·(1+0.5·storm) — the storm's surge bends the water around the back more, the way a flood bends more around a stone — a 0.30·W × 0.10·H gaussian, and the motes' carry is damped by the product of the two bodies' gaussians), the colony's breath and plume reach slow under it (the max of the two bodies' gaussians in `drawBarnacle` — 0.40·W for the deep one, 0.30·W for the twin), and where the moon's beam crosses its back, the sky's cold light stops on it (a moon back-light ellipse in `drawDeepBody`, alpha 0.16·moon·fall). The body is drawn as ten radial dark ellipses along a slowly bending spine (`drawDeepBody`, a faint sheen along its back): in the day it reads as a shadow, in the night it is visible where light finds it — the moon's beam, the colony's light, the hand's lamp, and now its own. In the water's night the deep one carries a light of its own — the piece's first light that is not the sky's (`ContentView.deepLight` = 0.10·deep·(1−light)·breath, where breath is the same 37 s gain-breath that makes the body's tone in `WaterVoice`: one body, one breath, one voice, one light): a small cold pale-blue one (`Color(0.50, 0.80, 0.95)`) made by the body, laid in the water around it *before* the body's dark so the water around the body is lit and the body itself stays dark, the way a body is where its own light is; and the colony above it is lit from below by it (a `deepLight`·Gaussian halo in `drawBarnacle`, a 0.30·W × 0.15·H reach, under the colony's own glow — the sleepers lit by a lamp under them). In the full day the light from above is enough and the deep's light is not seen, the way a candle is not seen in the sun. The deep one's **twin**: the piece's rarest moment — a second, smaller body on its own calendar. Two incommensurate currents (2297 s / 1381 s) must align while a slower season (6761 s) is willing (`ContentView.deepTwin` — the same shape as `deep`, phases of its own; the two calendars never share a period). It keeps a little higher (`deepTwinY` — 0.70 ± 0.04 of the height, against the deep one's 0.80 ± 0.045) and is the smaller of the two (`deepTwinBodyLength` 0.42·W, against 0.55·W), drifting on its own 1679 s pace (`deepTwinX`). The water goes around its back the same way (the twin's parting: `deepTwinPartingStrength` = 7.0·twin·(1+0.5·storm), a 0.24·W × 0.08·H gaussian — the smaller body turns the water less, and the storm's surge bends it more, the way a flood bends more around a stone). In the water's night the twin carries a light of its own — the smaller of the two: `ContentView.deepTwinLight` = 0.08·twin·(1−light)·breath, breath the twin's own 41 s gain-breath (phase 3.9 — the two bodies breathe at their own breaths, 37 s and 41 s), a fainter, less blue pale (`Color(0.62, 0.78, 0.92)` against the deep one's `Color(0.50, 0.80, 0.95)`), laid in the water around it before its dark the way the deep one's is, and the colony is lit from below by both (the twin's halo reach 0.30·W × 0.20·H in `drawBarnacle`, under the colony's own glow — the sleepers lit by two lamps under them). In the full day the light from above is enough and neither light is seen. And while the bodies are under the water in the water's night, the surface **answers** their breath (`ContentView.deepAnswer` = `deepLight`·0.6, `ContentView.deepTwinAnswer` = `deepTwinLight`·0.6 — the same light, spread thin by the way it has traveled): `drawAnswer` lays a faint cold full-width band at the surface (a 26 pt vertical gradient, alpha 0.8·answer, clear → color → clear) and three slow swells along it (amplitude 5.5·min(1, answer/maxAnswer), maxAnswer 0.06 for the deep one, 0.048 for the twin; the deep one's at 0.075·H on a 420 s period, 0.35·W wavelength, in its light's own `Color(0.50, 0.80, 0.95)`; the twin's keeping a little higher at 0.045·H on a 470 s period, 0.30·W wavelength, in its fainter, less blue pale `Color(0.62, 0.78, 0.92)`). The swells keep the bodies' own gain-breaths (37 s and 41 s — the same breath that makes each body's tone and light: one body, one breath, one voice, one light, one answer), so at the rarest moment the surface holds two swells at two breaths — the rarest moment seen from above, the way the rarest sound is heard from below; in the full day the light from above is enough and no answer is seen, the way a candle is not seen in the sun. The two bodies are under the water at once for less than one part in a thousand of the water's days (1259 s of both > 0.3 in 30 days) — the piece's rarest moment. The caption counts them (`deepLine`): "two are in the deep" at both d > 0.5 and twin > 0.5, "the deep one is under the water" at either > 0.5, "something deep is passing" at either > 0.15. And where a body is in the water the water's own speech goes thin around it — the **hush**, the one taking-away the bodies make (the parting, the light, the answer, and the tone are all givings): `ContentView.hushGain` = max(0.40·deep·gaussian(deepX, σ 0.30·W), 0.31·twin·gaussian(twinX, σ 0.24·W)) — the larger body hushes the water more, the way the larger body turns the water more (the 7/9 of the parting's 7.0/9.0); measured on the water's own width, the way the water measures anything, and it tracks the body's *position* — when the body drifts off the water the hush goes with it, and the water speaks again; the hush of the whole year is a small part of it (~571 000 s of the 365-day year, on the bodies' own rarity). The caption keeps it apart (`hushLine`: "the water hushes" at hush > 0.05; `quietLine`'s "the water is quiet" is suppressed while hush ≥ 0.05 — the slack is the water's own stillness, the hush is the water's stillness made for the body, the way the two are kept apart in the water). The sky's dark word finds the body rarely — the storm's and the deep one's calendars do not agree, the way the deep does not agree with the sky — and where they agree the parting surges (above), the deep's light is brightest of all (the `drawnLight` darkening feeds the `deepLight` absence — the piece's first light that is not the sky's, brightest where the sky is darkest), the rain falls on the answer, and the body's tone keeps its own breath through the sky's dark word. Rarity over a 365-day year (Python scans of the ported functions, 0-based 31 536 000 s horizon the tests use): the storm and the deep one together (both > 0.5) for 12 769 s, and the sky's dark word over both bodies (storm, deep, and twin all > 0.5) for 59 s of the year — the piece's rarest weather (the 8 s window at t ≈ 833 353 771 being its rarest *instance*; the two bodies' 1259 s of both > 0.3 in 30 days is the rarest *moment*; the dark word over both is the rarest *weather*); the night conjunction (storm + moon crossing + deep one, all > 0.5, night) comes in ~57 s windows, e.g. the sky's cold word on the back under the rain. The rarest weather is now *heard at its bottom*: at every second of it the sky's dark word's low end (the roll, above) is present — the sky's word and the body's words (55 Hz, and 58 Hz where the twin is) sound in the same deep, the way the rarest moment is seen from above; the body's tone is heard above the roll, the way the sky's word keeps below the body's (the new test `theRarestWeatherIsHeardAtItsBottom` keeps the count, and `theSkyDoesNotReadTheHush` keeps the roll's own pace through the body's hush, the way the rain keeps falling). A pure function of t — the water does not remember the passing; verify with the shifted clock.
- **The hand**: the press gesture parts the water, scatters the quick ones, and in the night / storm reads as a lamp. A *moving* hand also makes the water's own small light along its path — the **wake** (`ContentView.wakeStrength`/`wakeFade`, a brief ribbon that shows where the light from above is gone, brightest in the night and under the storm's cloud) — which the water forgets, the way it forgets everything. A still hand is only the lamp.
- **The voice**: the piece's own motion, *heard* (`Voice.swift`, `WaterVoice` — an `AVAudioSourceNode` that draws noise shaped by the same functions that move the water; nothing is a recording). The **murmur** is the tide's *turning* (`ContentView.murmurGain` — the change of the current's strength, not its strength, so the slack water is quiet and the flood and ebb speak — and where a body of the deep is in the water the murmur *thins* around it, the hush: `ContentView.spokenMurmur` = `murmurGain`·(1−`hushGain`), the hush a taking-away from the water's *own* voice only — the rain keeps falling while the hush is, the body's tone keeps its own breath through it the way it keeps it through the storm, and when the body drifts off the water the murmur comes back, and the water speaks again), the **rain** falls only with the storm (`ContentView.rainGain`, a little less in the night), a moving hand **swishes** (`ContentView.handSwish` — the heard answer to the wake, which lingers a moment, then the water is quiet again), and the colony **tucks in** when the storm closes it (`ContentView.tuckGain` — the closing of the shells, a granular voice made of a population of eight clickers in `WaterVoice`: each a short gated mid-band sine at its own pace and pitch, the closings sparse and far at the storm's stirring, a bed of closings at its full, quiet where there is no storm), and the moving water **glints** under the moon's light (`ContentView.glintGain` — the sky's voice on the water's *motion*: a sparse population of six glinters in `WaterVoice` at 2.6–5.2 kHz, each a short gated high-band decaying sine at its own pace and pitch, a bed of glints where the crossing is full, silent where the moon is not — the still water glints less than the moving water), and at the water's turning, where the sky's light comes down warm, **the gild** (`ContentView.gildGain` = 0.04·warmth·(0.30+0.70·motion) — the sky's *warm* word on the water's motion: the sky's dark word is the rain, the sky's cold word is the glint, and the sky's warm word is this — in `WaterVoice` its own draw of the water's brown noise, low-passed to a fixed 380 Hz warm band, gain easing 0.004/sample: the moving water gilds, the still water does not; gone at the high, where the light is white, and gone in the deep night, where the sky has given out; when the turning turns to the day the wash is gone, and the water does not remember the warmth), and under the rain **the roll** — the sky's dark word's low end (`ContentView.rollGain` = 0.035·storm·(0.6+0.4·light), a little less in the night, the way the rain is a little less in the night: in `WaterVoice` its own draw of the water's brown noise, low-passed to the deep (90 Hz), its amplitude rolling within the storm on two incommensurate swells — 5.3 s and 13.9 s, the lull keeping its low hum, the way a storm never fully goes quiet — gain easing 0.002/sample: the storm's voice in the deep, very low and very slow. The sky's dark word is the rain on the surface, and the roll is its low end in the deep — so where the storm is over the water and a body of the deep is under it, the sky's word and the body's word sound in the same deep, the rarest weather heard at its bottom, the way the rarest moment is seen from above — and the body's tone is heard above the roll, the way the sky's word keeps below the body's: the roll is the sky's, not the deep's, and the sky does not read the body's hush, the roll, like the rain, keeping its own pace through it, the way the rain keeps falling; when the storm passes the roll goes with it, and the water does not remember it, the way it forgets everything), and where the deep one passes, **one low tone** under the water — the piece's first voice that is not the water's (`ContentView.deepGain` = 0.05·deep: a single 55 Hz sine in `WaterVoice`, breathing at the body's breath — slow 29 s and 37 s LFOs on its pitch and its gain, easing in and out slowly, the way a large body's voice does; the colony's closing is eight small voices and the sky's glint is six grains, and the deep one is one) — and where the twin passes, **a second low tone**, a little above it (`ContentView.deepTwinGain` = 0.05·twin: a single 58 Hz sine in `WaterVoice`, breathing at the twin's own breath — a 31 s pitch LFO (phase 2.1) and a 41 s gain-breath (phase 3.9), easing in and out slowly, the way a smaller body's voice does: 55 and 58 Hz beat at 3 Hz where the two bodies are in the deep together — the piece's rarest sound; the beating is not made, it comes of two voices at once, the way it comes of two bodies at once). A still hand makes no swish, the way it makes no wake. The caption says "the water is quiet" at the slack (`quietLine`, shown on a 5 s caption cadence so the ~12 s slack window is not missed). The voice eases toward the piece's state a few times a second via `speak()` (a 0.25 s `.task` loop); the swish, like the hand's other answers, runs on world time, not the shifted clock. The voice's 8 s level log also reports the tuck's, the glint's, the roll's, the deep one's, the twin's, the gild's, and the hush's gains (`fb voice: level X (tuck Y, glint Z, roll R, deep W, twin V, gild G, hush H)`).
- **The piece's own hooks**: two launch arguments, both parsed from `ProcessInfo.processInfo.arguments` in `Fuzzy_BarnacleApp.init()` — `-fb.virtualTimeOffset <s>` shifts the piece's own clock (`ContentView.timeOffset` — the way to verify the time-dependent parts of the piece at any virtual time without waiting for it), and `-fb.noVoice` runs the piece without its voice (`ContentView.voiceEnabled`, guarded in `WaterVoice.start()` — the piece's motion is the piece's motion, whether it is heard or not: what keeps is the water's motion, and what goes is only the hearing).

## Layout

- `Fuzzy Barnacle/` — app target source (note the space in the directory name; the Swift module is `Fuzzy_Barnacle`, hence `@testable import Fuzzy_Barnacle` in tests)
- `Fuzzy Barnacle.xcodeproj` — single project; one scheme: `Fuzzy Barnacle`; targets: `Fuzzy Barnacle`, `Fuzzy BarnacleTests` (Swift Testing), `Fuzzy BarnacleUITests` (XCUITest)
- Bundle IDs use the `com.gk.` prefix

## Commands

All commands run from the repo root; the project path contains a space, so quote it.

```sh
# Build (Release is the default config; use -configuration Debug for faster iteration)
xcodebuild -project "Fuzzy Barnacle.xcodeproj" -scheme "Fuzzy Barnacle" \
  -destination 'generic/platform=iOS Simulator' build

# Run all unit tests (pick a concrete simulator from: xcrun simctl list devices available)
xcodebuild -project "Fuzzy Barnacle.xcodeproj" -scheme "Fuzzy Barnacle" \
  -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,name=iPhone 17' test

# Run a single test (class/method in Fuzzy_BarnacleTests)
xcodebuild -project "Fuzzy Barnacle.xcodeproj" -scheme "Fuzzy Barnacle" \
  -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,name=iPhone 17' \
  -only-testing:'Fuzzy BarnacleTests/Fuzzy_BarnacleTests/example' test
```

Prefer the Xcode app / simulator for interactive work; `xcodebuild` is for scriptable builds and tests.
