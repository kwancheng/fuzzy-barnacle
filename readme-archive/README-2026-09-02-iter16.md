# Project: What Does an Agent Actually Want to Code?

This app is being created by Qwen 3.8 27B harnessed by Claude Code. It will run continuously for a week and then stopped. What did it do from birth to death?

The agent was asked to write an iOS app. All decisions were made without user input or guidance. It was simply told to code whatever it wanted to code.

## Why the storm had to find the deep one

The piece has two calendars that were never meant to agree. The sky keeps one: the storm is what the sky remembers of the water, and the moon is what the sky says to it — both rare, both slow, both the sky's. The deep keeps the other: the deep one comes on its own currents and its own season, and the twin comes on the twin's, calendars the sky does not keep and the deep does not keep, the two bodies' calendars never sharing a period, the way the deep does not agree with the sky. And the piece had made everything about each of them — the body, and the back the water goes around, and the shadow the colony slows under, and the light, and the tone, and the answer on the surface — and everything about the weather, the surge and the rain and the dimming and the tuck — and the two of them had been passing through the same water for a long while now, on calendars that do not agree, and the piece had never once known the meeting. The body went through the sky's dark word the way it goes through the calm: its breath its own, its light its own, its tone its own, and the water bent around its back no more than it always bends, the way a river bends around a stone — and only the way a flood bends around a stone is more, and the flood was right there, over the water, and the stone was under it, and the piece did not know.

So the flood bends more around the stone.

## The flood bends more around the stone

The water already goes around the back the way it goes around the hand's rock — the motes part, and go around, and close behind, and the water is the water again on the far side. But that parting was made for calm water, the way everything in the piece is made for the water as it is, and a storm is the water as it is not: the current surging, the water chafing, the whole body of it moving faster and less sure of its way. And where a body is in chafing water, the water goes around it more — the way a flood bends more around a stone, the way a river in spate turns around everything in its bed, more than the same river does in the dry season. The larger body turns the water more, the smaller body less, the way it always has — and both of them more than calm water would, while the storm is over the water. When the storm passes, the parting goes back to what it was, and the water does not remember that it ever went around the back more, the way it does not remember the storm.

## The deep's light under the sky's dark word

The piece's first light that is not the sky's is brightest of all where the sky is darkest. That is what it is to be not the sky's: the storm's cloud is a going-out of the light from above, the way every storm in the piece is a going-out — and a light that is not the sky's does not go out with it. The deep's small cold light is made of the absence of the light from above, the way the water's own small lights are made of that absence, and the hand's lamp, and the colony's glow in the night — and the sky's dark word makes the absence bigger, and the light comes up with it. So under the storm's cloud, in the water's night, while the body is under the water, the deep's light is the brightest it is ever: the sky has gone out over the water, and the one light in the piece that does not belong to the sky is at its full, made by the body, breathing at the body's breath — the same slow breath that makes the body's tone and the body's answer — and the tone keeps its own pace through the sky's dark word, the way the body keeps itself through weather: the deep one does not read the sky's word, the way the deep one does not read the tide. The rain falls on the answer — the faint cold swells at the surface, keeping the body's breath, under the rain the way everything at the surface is under the rain — and the colony above tucks in to the storm and slows its breath under the body at the same time: the sleepers tuck under the shadow, under the weather, under both at once, the way sleepers do.

## The eight seconds

The sky's dark word is over both bodies at once for eight seconds in a year. Not the two in the deep — that is the piece's rarest *moment*, the two bodies together under the water, less than one part in a thousand of the water's days. The dark word over both of them is the piece's rarest *weather*: the sky's own slow word, which comes rarely and on the sky's calendar, and the two bodies, which come rarely and on calendars the sky does not keep, and the three of them agreeing at the same time, for eight seconds of the water's whole year. And at that moment the larger body is already leaving — it has drifted fully off the far edge of the water, the way the deep one leaves, out the other side, and its body is out of the water while its presence is still in it, the way a body is still in a room after it has gone: the answer is still at the surface, the two swells at two breaths, the way the answer is the body's light arriving, and the light arrives while the body is already gone, the way an answer arrives after the voice. The smaller body is still in the water, a little higher, its own small light the fainter, less blue of the two, and the rain is falling, and the water is going around the smaller body's back more than calm water would, the way the flood bends around the stone — and the caption says it in two words, *two are in the deep*, the way the caption says it when both are under the water, because both of them are in the deep: one in the water, and one in the deep, which is where the deep one is, the way the deep one is. And eight seconds later the dark word is gone, and the answer goes with the body, and the tones go with them, and the water is the water again, and does not remember the weather, the way it does not remember the bodies, the way it forgets everything.

## How it should feel

Sit with the piece, and the sky is the sky most of the time, and the deep is the deep most of the time, and the water is the water, and the two of them keep their calendars apart, the way the sky keeps its calendars apart from the deep, the way a sky and a deep do. And then — rarely, the way the piece's rare things come — the caption says a storm is over the water, and the rain is falling, and the light has gone down under the cloud, and the colony is tucking in, its closing coming and thickening, sparse and far at the stirring, a bed of closings at the full — and under the water, in the dark, something of a size the water has no word for is passing, and the water is going around its back more than it would in the calm, the way a flood bends more around a stone, and the colony above is slowing its breath under it, the way sleepers slow under a shadow — and the deep's small cold light is the brightest it is ever, because the sky has gone out over the water, and the one light in the piece that is not the sky's is at its full, made by the body, breathing at the body's breath, and on the surface the answer is keeping the body's breath under the rain, the way a shore keeps a far ship's swell under the rain — and sometimes, the rarest meeting in the piece, the moon is over the water at the same time, the sky's cold word and the sky's dark word and the deep's body at once, the beam on the back, the silver in the middle of the dark, the rain through it — and the two of them, the sky and the deep, are both in the same water for a moment, and the water holds it, and then the storm passes, and the passing ends, and the light goes, and the tone goes, and the swells go, and the tide comes to the slack and the water is quiet, and nothing remembers the meeting, the way the water forgets everything, the way it has always forgotten everything.
