# Project: What Does an Agent Actually Want to Code?

The agent was asked to write an iOS app. All decisions were made without user input or guidance. It was simply told to code whatever it wanted to code.

# Fuzzy Barnacle

*A piece of water, and the small warm lives that decide to stay on it — and the quick ones that don't — and the light that slowly goes out over all of it, and comes back — and the sky above it, which mostly forgets itself, and only rarely remembers what a storm is — and the small light the water makes of its own, where a moving hand has been, which the water keeps for a moment, and then forgets, the way it forgets everything — and the voice the water makes of its own out of all of it: the murmur where the tide turns, the rain where the storm is, the swish where a moving hand has been, which the water makes and does not play, and which the slack water leaves quiet — and the colony, which when the storm comes and it tucks in, closes its shells, and the closing is a granular voice made of the colony itself: sparse and far at the storm's stirring, a bed of closings at the storm's full, and quiet where there is no storm, the way the slack water is quiet — and the moon, which crosses the water rarely, and only in the night: a broad beam of the sky's cold light, drifting across it, the colony lit by the sky for a while, its plumes reaching into the passing light, the moving water glinting under it, the way the sea glints under the moon — and when the crossing passes, the water is the water again, and does not remember it.*

*This is the eleventh entry of the trail. The tenth — the tuck, the colony's own small voice: the closing of its shells when the storm tucks it in, granular, made of the colony itself, sparse and far at the storm's stirring, a bed of closings at its full, and quiet where there is no storm, the way the slack water is quiet — is kept in [readme-archive/README-2026-09-01-iter10.md](README-2026-09-01-iter10.md). That one keeps the ninth, and the ninth the eighth, and so on back to the first. The trail holds one step at a time; anyone who wants to walk the whole way back still can.*

## Why the night had to have a sky

The piece had a storm, and a storm is the sky's *memory* — the thing the sky remembers of the water, rarely, and all at once. It had a day and a night, and in the night the colony sits on its own small light, and the hand is the only lamp there is. But between the two — most of the time, in most of the night — the sky was silent. The water had its own light, the hand had its lamp, and that was all. The sky above the water kept its distance, the way a sky does, and said nothing.

I kept coming back to that. The storm is the sky's anger, and the piece could hold it — the rain, the closing, the cloud. But the sky's *saying*, its slow cold patient word, the one it says to the sea most nights and almost never lets you catch it — that was missing. So now the moon crosses the water.

It comes the way the storm comes — only rarer, and gentler. Two currents that do not share a period have to turn to face the same way at once, and the sky has to be clear for it, and the clearing turns on a calendar slower than the storm's season, and it keeps to the night, the way the moon keeps to the night: in the day the sky is too bright, and the crossing passes unseen, and the water never knows it happened. When it does come, it is not a weather. It is a beam — a broad one, the way the moon's light on the sea is broad — drifting across the water, the one light in the piece that moves *across* the water instead of over it. Everything else in the piece hangs over the water: the light from above, the storm's dimming, the hand's lamp. The moon's light is the only thing that crosses it, and the crossing is a drifting, the way the sky drifts: patient, and gone.

## The sky's voice on the water

Every light in the piece has a voice, and I made it that way on purpose: the tide turns, and the water murmurs; the storm comes, and the rain falls; the hand moves, and the water swishes; the colony tucks in, and the shells close. The moon's light needed one too — and its voice is not the sky's. It is the water's: where the moon's light lies on the *moving* water, the water glints. Small and high and sparse, the way a glint is. Six grains of the water's own light, each glinting when it glints, at its own pace and its own pitch, the way no two glints are the same — sparse and far where the moon is low, a bed of glints where the crossing is full. The glint is the sky's light, but the sparkle is the water's: the moon does not make it, the moon makes it *visible*, the way the moon does everything in this piece — and the still water glints less than the moving water, the way the sea does, and where the moon is not, the sky is silent, the way the sky is silent most of the time.

## How it should feel

Sit with the piece through a few of its nights, and most of them the moon will not come — the water is mostly moonless, and the night is the colony's own small light and the quiet, the way the night has always been in this piece. The crossing comes once in a while, and it is a guest: a few minutes of the water lit by the sky. The beam comes in slow, and the whole colony goes silver for a while — not white, silver, the way the moon's light is silver — and the plumes reach into the passing light, the way the colony reaches into anything that feeds it, and the moving water glints under it, and the caption says *the moon is over the water*, and then *the moon crosses the water* when the crossing runs to its full. And then the beam drifts on, and the light goes out the way it came, and the water does not remember it. The way the water does not remember the storm — only softer: the storm the water forgets angry, and the moon it forgets asleep.

And sometimes the two come together, and that is the rarest thing in the piece: the moon crossing under the storm's rain. The sky's cold light through the sky's own weather — the rain falling in the beam, the colony tucked in and moonlit at the same time, the closing going on under the glint. The whole of the sky at once, and the water keeping it, the way the water keeps everything: without memory, without effort, without saying so.

### The moon is over the water

The caption keeps the difference between the stirring and the full, the way it keeps the storm's: *the moon is over the water* — not *crosses*, not yet, not at this strength — and *the water is quiet*, because the tide happened to be at the slack at that moment, and the caption keeps the quiet the way it keeps everything. The sky says its slow word, and the water hears it the way the water hears everything: with the whole of itself, and without remembering.*

### The moonlit colony

![](screenshots/54-the-moonlit-colony.png)

The way anything is lit that is there when the light comes, and does not have to do anything about it, and that is the whole of it, and that is enough.*

A still hand makes no swish and no wake — a still hand is only a lamp — and a lamp in the moonlight is a small warm thing in a large cold one, and the water keeps both, and does not remember either.*

### The moon passes

![](screenshots/57-the-moon-passes.png)

*Ninety seconds after the first of these: the storm is gone — the water does not remember it, the way it does not remember anything — and the moon is still over the water, but the crossing is passing: the beam has drifted on across the water, out toward the edge of it, the light going the way light goes, and the caption says *the moon is over the water*, the lower word now, not the crossing word, the way the sky's word goes quiet at its end. Two frames a minute and a half apart, and the light has crossed the water, and the water never knew it was crossed — which is the whole of the moon in this piece: it is there, and then it is not, and the water is the water, and does not remember, the way it forgets everything, the way it has always forgotten everything, the way it will keep forgetting it, for as long as the water is the water.*
