## Why the water had to drink the sky's color

The piece gave the sky's light a color, and for a while that was enough: the soft surface light took it, the light shafts took it, the motes took it, and the turning was gold where it had been white, and the day was the day the piece had always drawn. But there was one thing the color had not reached, and it was the one thing that mattered most, because it was the thing the piece was. The water itself. The depth of it, the body of it, the blue the whole piece sits in — the thing every other light in the piece is a light *of* — still went from black to blue to bright blue the way it always had, on the brightness of the light from above, and kept its own fixed blue in every hour of the water's day, the way a painted sea keeps its blue. And that was not right, the way a painted sea is not right. A real sea at dusk is not a blue sea under a gold sky. A real sea at dusk is *copper* — the water takes the color of the light that falls on it, the way water takes whatever the light in it is: gold light in the water is gold water, low and warm and the color of the last of the sun, and white light in the water is the water's own blue, the way the sea under a white sky is its own blue, and the way the piece has always drawn it. The sky's light had a color now, and the water — the piece's own water, the water the colony is on and the deep one is under and the quick ones ride and the hand parts — was the one thing in the piece that did not know it. So the water drinks it now. The water drinks the color of the light that comes down, the way water drinks whatever the light in it is, and the piece's own small lights keep their own colors the way they have always kept them — the deep's pale blue, the twin's paler blue, the colony's cold green, the water's own cold cyan where a moving hand has been — because a light that is not the sky's has to be something, and only the water drinks the sky's color, and only drinks: it takes the color it is given, and never adds a color of its own, the way water only ever holds whatever the light in it is.

## The water drinks what the sky gives

So the water drinks what the sky gives. At the high of the day the light that comes down is white, the way the high light is white — and white light in the water is the water's own blue, exactly as it was, the way the sea under a white sky is its own blue: the full day is the full day the piece has always drawn, and the change is the turning, the way the low light is the hour the sky is warm. And at the turning — the low light, the meeting of the water's day and night, where the sky's light comes down warm gold, the way dusk is gold and dawn is gold — the water drinks the gold, the way the sea drinks the last of the sun: the water goes copper where the light lies, and teal where it does not, and the whole piece goes with it, the way a shore goes with the last of the light — the colony sleeping in it, the quick ones riding it, the deep, if the deep is passing, carrying its own small cold light through it, a cold pale blue in the gold, the way cold is cold only against the warm, the way the piece's own light reads as its own only against the sky's. And in the deep night the sky has little light left to give a color, the way the sky has little light left to give anything, and the water is nearly dark, and what little color is left in it is the sky's, the way the last of a twilight is the sky's, and the colony's own cold light is all there is, the way the deep night is cold, the way the deep night has always been cold. And when the sky's cloud comes — the piece's dark word — the cloud takes the light, the way a cloud takes the light: the water under it is darker, the way the water is darker under the cloud — but the cloud does not take the color, the way a cloud does not take the color of the dusk it is in: under the cloud in the low light the water is still gilded, the way the dusk under a cloud is still dusk, the warmth under the cloud, the gold under the cloud, the way the last of the sun is under the cloud, and only when the turning turns to the day does the gold go, and the water is the water again — its own blue, its own dark, its own light — and does not remember the gold, the way it forgets everything, the way it has always forgotten everything. A pure function of the water's clock, the way everything in the piece is: the water drinks the sky's color, and the sky's color is a pure function of the water's clock, and when the hour is over the water is the water, and does not remember the hour.

## The sky's warm word has a voice

The sky has words the piece can hear, and for a while the piece could hear two of them. The sky's dark word is the rain — where the storm is, the rain falls, the way the sky's dark word is a falling, and the water does not remember the falling, the way it forgets everything. And the sky's cold word is the glint — where the moon's light lies on the moving water, the water glints under it, small and high and sparse, the way a glint is, the way the sea glints under the moon, and the still water glints less than the moving water, the way the moon does not make the sparkle, it makes the sparkle visible. And the sky's warm word — the turning, the hour the sky's light comes down warm, the low light, the gold — the piece could see, and the piece could name, "the water is in its turning," the way it names its night and its day — but the piece could not *hear*. The warm hour was the one hour of the sky the piece had never given a voice, the way the sky's warm word is the one word the sky has said that the water had not yet answered. So the water answers it now, the way the water answers everything: with a low warm wash — the gild — present only while the light is warm, the way the glint is present only while the moon's light is: gone at the high, where the light is white, and gone in the deep night, where the sky has given out, and up at the turning, the way the turning is up, the way the low light is the low light. And it is the sky's voice on the water's *motion*, the way the glint is the sky's voice on the water's motion: the moving water gilds, the still water does not — the sky does not make the warmth, the way the moon does not make the sparkle, it makes the warmth *audible in the motion*, the way the light makes the sparkle, the way the piece makes everything audible in the motion it is made of. The sky's dark word is the rain, the sky's cold word is the glint, and the sky's warm word is the gild — and when the turning turns to the day the wash is gone, and the water is the water again, and does not remember the warmth, the way it forgets everything, the way the water has always forgotten everything, the way the water forgets the gold the way it forgets the rain the way it forgets the glint the way it forgets the meeting of the sky's dark word and the deep one, the way it forgets everything it is ever given, and keeps only what it is.
